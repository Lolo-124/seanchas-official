The Washerwoman

He needed to be quick.

Squatting down as if to scoop the laundry from her washing basket, Kester slid between her and the river. She squawked in anger and beat a fist into his shoulder, but he twisted his feet into the stones to brace himself.

Grabbing her wrists and pinning them to her sides, Kester shut his eyes and prayed fervently for any kind of help. The old crone went still and with care, Kester straightened his posture out. Her little teeth flashed again as she met his eyes.

“Well caught,” she rasped, and now he could hear the magic in her voice.

The stories must be true. If she’s Bean Nighe and you stand between her and her water, then you get one honest request and an honest answer in return. If she’s human, then you get beat half to death and your mother thrashes you for harassing an old lady. His heart was pounding and his grip on her wrists was slippery.

“Ask me,” she said, jolting Kester back to reality. “I haven’t got all day.”
