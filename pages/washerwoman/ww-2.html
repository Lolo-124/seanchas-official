The Washerwoman

He swallowed the shake in his voice and reached for his best manners.

“I am looking for something. Please, ma’am, where did you get that jumper?”

She cackled, and it rang in his ears and through the trees, but she did not answer. She folded down again to wash and as she did the water ran darker in the burn.

Kester’s tongue felt glued to the roof of his mouth and the young tree branches around him whipped into his body as the wind picked up.

The old woman collected her washing basket and heaved herself to her feet, wading directly into the burn as though it were a dream. Kester could feel the compulsion to follow her twisting down his spine. The trees thrashed in the wind. He felt dizzy.

Bean Nighe. She must be.

He raised his arm to cross himself and felt his foot lift against his will.
