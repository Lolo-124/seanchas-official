The Washerwoman

Kester’s fears about a curse never quite came true.

At least not in the way he pictured.

He didn’t die a grisly death, he didn’t fail his exams, and when he asked Lucy to the dance a few years later, she agreed.

But in the evenings or on cloudy days, he could see a faint glow around people. It was stronger on the young ones and dimmed in those who were sickly. Sometimes he caught a flash of red in the light around people, but he didn’t understand what that meant until one day when his mum took ill and for a moment the light around her flickered red.

That flickering came increasingly often until the whole of her glow was red and dimming. She lay weakly in her bed, eyes closed, pale fingers clasped gently around each other.

Kester understood the curse he’d been given all those years ago. He would know when others were near death, and he would be unable to tell them.
