The Washerwoman

Kester was dizzy with his fear and choked with worry for Davie.

He felt anger rise and gave her arms a shake.

“I need my brother back. You have him! Give him back to me!”

Her laugh was dry and crackled like kindling.

“That can be managed. I need just wring the clothing dry and my mark will wash off of him. But it must lie with someone. Who will take it up?”

She wrenched her bony wrists free from him and stepped aside to snatch the sodden jumper up. With quick deft twists she crushed the water from it and held the garment out to Kester.

“The choice is yours, dearie.”

Kester’s heart beat so hard he could hear it rushing in his ears. Someone needed to take the jumper.

Did it have to be him? Maybe it didn’t need to be worn right away. He could set the jumper on the side of the path, and whoever walked past next and wore it would be the one to be cursed. That was unkind, he thought, but maybe no one ever would and the curse would fade to nothing.

Trying to thwart a curse never ended well in the stories he had overheard though. It frightened him terribly, but maybe he needed to take the jumper himself to make sure Davie was all right. Besides, he was the older brother. His job was to look out for his sibling.
