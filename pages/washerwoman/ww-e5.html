The Washerwoman

Kester refused her hand.

He wasn’t sure how she would react to that. His heart beat a tattoo against his ribs.

Her eyes narrowed and her posture straightened out.

“If you won’t come with me you will have to go back to where you came from.”

Although Kester was relieved to hear it, her next words sent a chill through his already frozen, sodden body.

“I will be with you constantly. You are what was owed to me, and the magic of that fulfilled promise is bound to your soul now. Though you can never return to me, you will always be drawn to this place.”

Kester worried about what she said the entire walk home. Since the sun had set, and she seemed to think that his going home would be a worse fate than staying with her, she had sent him off with a rowan branch and a promise not to look behind himself until he crossed the threshold of his home.

He was greeted at the door by Davie and Mum, and relief washed over him so strongly that tears pricked in his eyes. He scrubbed his eyes furtively on his sleeve. He was too old to cry and the warmth of the house made the evening feel like an unwelcome dream.

The feeling lasted until, as Kester swung the door shut behind him, he saw dozens of glowing little eyes peering out at him from the darkness. Shuddering, he turned back to his family and went to bed grateful to be safe, but still concerned about what the Bean Nighe had said.

The morning began as usual until the first time he ran the taps. The water echoed deafeningly in his head. Instead of the usual splashing, it sounded like the repetitive slap of fabric on a washboard. He felt the hair on the back of his neck prick up and swung his head around. He was alone in the washroom, but he could feel the presence of that woman’s uncanny stare on him.

As the day went on he realized that any running water sounded like a washboard, and it echoed unnaturally in his ears. Always, always it came with the knowledge that he was being watched.
