The Washerwoman

His feet dragged him down the rest of the bank and into the burn.

The water was icy, and the pressure on his boots made it hard to balance in the rushing flow. Kester’s heart beat like a rabbit in his chest and though he tried desperately to shake off whatever compulsion the Bean Nighe had placed over him, he couldn’t keep himself from wading deeper into the burn after her fading figure.

She was awfully fast for an old woman. Kester caught glimpses of her through the mist but couldn’t catch up. The water at his torso was so cold that he felt his stomach burn inside him as he sloshed behind her.

He dragged himself dripping and shivering from the water on the far side of the burn. As he slogged after her the river water in his wellies slopped out onto the mossy ground. He tried to call out to her. His mouth felt sewn shut.

Finally he could make out her form, still as a statue next to a cairn, the pale stones shining dully through the mirk. The washing basket rested on her hip. The wind trailed up his spine and stole the last of the heat from his hands. Still his feet followed the old crone until he stood on the other side of the cairn from her.

He found his voice for a moment. “Please, my brother.”

“He’s safe at home, my child. Dinna fash.”

The hand she extended to him was pallid, wrinkled and wet. He saw the webs between her fingers again and his stomach turned over.

Did she mean for him to take her hand? What would come of it if he turned it down?
