The Washerwoman

“Why his jumper?” Kester’s voice sounded thin and reedy, and he cleared his throat and asked again.

He didn’t give the woman Davie’s name.

“There is a debt owed to me.”

“My brother can’t owe you anything, he’s only ten.”

“Not him specifically, but your family does.” Her brow furrowed and her teeth poked out again as she grinned. Her breath stank as it fanned over Kester’s face. “Your grandfather’s grandfather took a coo from the hill and never paid the teind, and the hill has waited for a child of that house with a good heart.”

“So you just nabbed him?” Kester felt a bit lightheaded.

“He has a bit too much spirit, I find. No, no, deary,” and on the endearment her voice grew sickly sweet, “he was only the hook. He will do, but I’ll only let him go if I’m given you.”
