Glossary

amn’t
“am not” (“I amn’t sure”)
baffies
house slippers
bairn
child
baltic
freezing cold
Bean Nighe
“washing woman” (roughly ben-NEE-uh), a Highland fairy who washes the clothes of those about to die
Beltane
the May Day fire festival, when the fair folk are most active and fires are lit to protect homes and livestock
birk
birch tree
brash
loose dry twigs and clippings
burn
small stream
cairn
a heap of stones used as a landmark or grave marker
canna
can’t
chuffed
pleased, proud of oneself
coo
cow
doddery
shaky or frail with age
fash / dinna fash
to trouble or worry; “dinna fash” means “don’t worry”
football
soccer
geas
a magical obligation or taboo laid on someone
gey unco
“gey” is very or rather, and “unco” is strange or uncanny, so “something gey unco” means something rather odd
glen
narrow valley (from gleann)
gloaming
dusk, evening twilight
haar
cold sea fog on the east coast
hen
a term of address for a girl or woman, like “love” or “dear”
the host
the fairy procession that rides out on feast nights
jumper
sweater
knackered
exhausted
knowe
small hill or knoll (rhymes with “now”)
legged it
ran off
lovebugs
St Mark’s flies, small black flies that fly coupled together in spring
mirk
darkness, a variant of “murk”
nicked
stolen
row
a telling-off (rhymes with “cow”)
rowan
mountain ash, a tree traditionally protective against fairies
seanchas
Gaelic lore, traditional knowledge and storytelling (roughly SHEN-uh-khus, with the kh as in “loch”)
skooshed
squirted or shot out quickly
tea
the evening meal as well as the drink
tea towel
dish towel
teind
the tithe or tribute certain groups of the fairies owe to hell every seven years, or the tithe one owes the fair folk if caught robbing them
telly
TV
thick ear
a clip or slap round the ear
Vicks
menthol vapour rub to soothe congestion
wee
small
wellies
short for Wellington boots, which are rubber rain boots
