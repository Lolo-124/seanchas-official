The Birch Girl

Eilidh felt her heart give a little.

The stories said that’s how you had to treat the fair folk, but they were beings too. Shouldn’t she try to be kind?

She grabbed the fallen tea towel from where it had dropped out of her bag, and climbed up to sit on the lowest branch of the tree. She pressed the tea towel hard against the cut on the girl’s waist.

Her eyes opened. She looked at Eilidh from beneath her eyelids and whispered, “The fallen wood is too far gone. I still have some time. Stay with me a minute.”

The sun was nearly down. The dim light made the birch girl’s skin look even paler.

“How can I help?”

“The water from the stream at my roots. Can you bring me some to drink?”

“I have water in my bottle? It’s a plastic bottle I think.”

“It must be water from the stream. Yours has been through metal pipes.”

Eilidh waited till the girl’s hand was secure over the towel, then dropped back to the ground to get her water bottle. She tipped the water out and walked to the far side of the tree.

Sure enough, there was a little rivulet of water flowing through a channel right at the base of the roots. Eilidh sat with the bottle’s mouth under the water for a while, then carried the cold bottle back up to the fairy tree.

Into the gash in the tree’s trunk that was her mouth, Eilidh poured the water. The birch drank sips at first, then deeper as she started to heal.

It was pitch black now. Bells started to ring from deeper in the forest, and Eilidh was torn. She had stayed too long. She had to go.

Then up the path a light shone. Wondering, Eilidh watched as a procession of riders came through, set astride pale horses and carrying lanterns, their hair unbound. Some wore cloaks. At the end of the line Eilidh saw a silhouette she knew.

Gran. Eilidh felt the breath go out of her. She called out, but Gran didn’t turn or acknowledge her. The procession continued on up the trail, and eventually the old woman was swallowed by the forest.

The fairy next to her stirred. “The weaker ones are taken first,” she murmured, “I’m sorry.”
