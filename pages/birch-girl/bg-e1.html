The Birch Girl

Gran lived a good many more years after that Beltane.

If nothing else Eilidh had that.

Eilidh had woken the next morning with sap sticky on her cheek. Her scalp itched. When she ran her hands through her hair, her fingers caught in the leaves growing there. Thin, needle-thin branches grew where her hair had been, and leaves budded and opened while she watched, no bigger than a mouse’s ear.

She had panicked, and when trying to pull them out gave her splitting headaches, she had shorn everything off with the kitchen scissors. The next morning they had grown back.

Mum tried to get her in to see a specialist, but the NHS were wildly unhelpful. Gran eventually talked them out of trying. Modern science would be no help. It was a magical consequence.

In autumn they turned gold and littered the ground where she walked. In spring the new buds got sap onto everything.

The old birch in the ditch never bore leaves again, but Eilidh did.
