The Birch Girl

“An armful, if that’s all right,” Eilidh said.

It was Gran. Better to have a bit too much than far too little.

“That’s enough,” the girl said gently when Eilidh’s arms were full, but Eilidh reached up for just one more bough in case, and then one more.

The birch tree’s colour dulled. Her hair was now barren and stripped. The last of the twigs came away, and her eyes closed. Then her face stilled. She did not speak again. It was only a tree.

The sprigs went up at the first scrape of the lighter, curling back on themselves like paper, and the flame ran up the pile hot and fast. The fire burned the brightest the glen had seen in a hundred years. None of the fair folk came.

Eilidh carried what was left of the birch tree’s hair down the hill in her arms and set it by the back door. She went up to bed with ash in her jacket cuffs and the smell of smoke in her hair.
