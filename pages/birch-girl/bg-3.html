The Birch Girl

In the dark, with branches pressing in on her,

she felt suddenly claustrophobic and swung the knife out against the tree limbs in blind panic. They shook, but the knife only caught in the branches. Eilidh wrestled it free, then hacked at the thinner ones closer to the bottom of the cage. They snapped, broke, or tore free from the tree and Eilidh clawed her way out, heart jackhammering against her ribs.

She fled up the bank of the ditch and across the path, out of reach of the flailing tree limbs, tripping over the branches she’d cut loose.

She lay waiting for the tree to still its thrashing. She could hear the girl crying; it was a thin whimper and the tree’s leaves shook in time with her heaving breaths. Eventually it fell still.

Eilidh crept closer. The sun was setting. She had to decide. The girl listed slightly, one limb pressed against her waist where the blade had cut, and silver tear tracks shone on her cheeks even in the evening light. Her eyes were fast shut.

With trepidation Eilidh lifted the branches closest to her off the ground and cradled them in the crook of her arm. She crept closer and closer to the ditch’s edge, reaching down to gather as many as she could reach.

As she extended herself down the slope, her balance tipped and her bag slipped up her back and knocked her in the head. She tumbled down into the ditch in a cacophony and leapt to her feet, looking around for her knife, but the girl in the tree didn’t even open her eyes.
