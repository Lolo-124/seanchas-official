The Birch Girl

“Time,” Eilidh said.

“How much time?”

“Seven years.” The birch’s rasping voice seemed to echo. “Light your fire first, then return to me.”

The branches around her shook down the dry and dead from among them, and Eilidh gathered them up, running up to the knowe to light the fire before night fell. The lighter scraped twice before it took, and then the bark caught, curling back on itself like paper. The smoke was thin and white and sweet, and there was enough wood there that Eilidh knew it would burn fine all night.

She walked back down the path. A bargain is a bargain, but for a moment Eilidh thought about heading home without stopping by the birch again. When she tried to take a different path, though, she found her feet would not obey, and she realized that a geas had been placed on her.

She made it back to the birch and took her hand. The branches closed in on her.

It felt like one night. When the branches lifted in the morning, Eilidh made her way back to the house. She did not recognize the car in the drive. Her key didn’t fit the lock. Mum opened the door.

There was grey in her hair that hadn’t been there at breakfast. She cried, clasping Eilidh close to her, and it took a minute for the tears to stop. “You’ve not grown,” she said, and held Eilidh tightly for a long, long time.

Over tea she caught Eilidh up. Gran had gone in her sleep three winters back. Dad had lit the Beltane fire every spring since. Eilidh’s friends were grown, with cars and jobs, and she was exactly as old as she’d been that morning.
