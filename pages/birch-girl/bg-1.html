The Birch Girl

Eilidh reached for her best manners and swallowed the shake in her throat.

Slowly she lowered the knife to the roots and stood up with her hands open.

“Begging your pardon, miss, but I’d like to ask something of you. My Gran’s ill, and I need birk for a Beltane fire. I’d rather ask than take.” Eilidh wasn’t sure if that was wise, considering everything the old fogies said, but the idea of demanding something felt too foreign.

The wood creaked as the birch girl smiled. “No one has asked me anything in years.”

A breeze shook the wood, and the air filled with the smell of sweet sap.

“I cannot give you living branches, but I can give you the hair from my head. A gift lies heavy on the giver, lass, and heavier on the one who takes. Are you sure you’d have it?”

Eilidh held her breath and nodded once.

The birch girl lifted her wooden hand and plucked a reedy branch from the wood above her head, and held it out to Eilidh.

She could feel every old warning ringing in her head. Never take what is given, never owe one of the Folk, beware the kindest ones the most. The sun was starting to set. Her hands felt clammy.

Take what is offered Refuse the gift
